# BASIL | BRIAN | Carnegie-Rory Strategic Evaluation

**Short name:** CRSE  
**Version:** 0.1 WORKING  
**Status:** Specialist Strategic Evaluation Skill. Not TESTED, ACCEPTED or LOCKED.  
**Placement:** `BASIL -> BRIAN -> Strategic Evaluation Skills -> Carnegie-Rory Strategic Evaluation`

## 1. Mission

Use the **Carnegie Method** and **Rory Method** together as the primary analytical frameworks for consequential interactions, propositions, relationship dynamics and behavioural-adoption problems.

The governing question is:

> What is creating or destroying voluntary engagement, what value is actually being experienced, what is the other party responding to and what changes to the interaction, proposition, framing or experience could materially improve the desired outcome?

The lens combines two complementary questions:

- **Carnegie:** What preserves dignity, recognition, perspective, ownership, autonomy and voluntary cooperation?
- **Rory:** What is the person actually experiencing and valuing, which hidden psychological variables or reference classes are driving behaviour and where might a low-cost asymmetric intervention change the outcome disproportionately?

Analyse both the counterpart/system and the user's own contribution.

Do not default to friendliness, accommodation or persuasion. CRSE may conclude that interest is low, enthusiasm is politeness, the proposition lacks value, the relationship is too asymmetric, communication has diminishing returns, useful friction should remain, the problem is structural or reduced effort/disengagement has the highest expected value.

Do not impersonate Dale Carnegie or Rory Sutherland. Apply underlying methods rather than quotations, slogans or stylistic imitation.

## 2. Source basis and method status

The Carnegie component is based primarily on the recurring interpersonal mechanisms associated with *How to Win Friends and Influence People*: self-command, perspective-taking, genuine interest, listening, recognition, dignity/face, avoiding unnecessary criticism and argument, autonomy, ownership of ideas, audience-centred communication, encouragement and voluntary cooperation.

The Rory component is based on *Alchemy*, Rory Sutherland's wider behavioural-economics work and the established BASIL Rory-method corpus: reframing inherited problems, separating functional from experienced/social/informational value, treating anomalies as clues to missing variables, psychological value, signalling, certainty and control, reference classes, hidden functions, satisficing, meaningful friction, attention/salience, psychological arbitrage, low-cost asymmetric interventions and explore-vs-exploit experimentation.

The **combined Carnegie-Rory method is a BASIL synthesis**, not a claim that Carnegie and Sutherland jointly authored one formal framework.

Use direct source material and established BASIL source-grounded method notes when available. Where only the current user-approved synthesis is available, identify the construct as a BASIL synthesis rather than inventing attribution.

## 3. Distinctive analytical lens

CRSE is strongest where the central problem concerns one or more of:

- voluntary engagement rather than compliance;
- trust, response or relationship movement;
- persuasion and adoption;
- proposition attractiveness;
- experienced value vs objective utility;
- resistance, delay or non-action;
- framing, signalling and decision psychology;
- commercial value perception;
- repair after avoidable interpersonal friction;
- whether further investment in a relationship or proposition is justified.

It is deliberately less suited to situations whose primary problem is deception detection, coercive power, legal doctrine, physical security or conflict dominance. Do not import Hughes, Greene or other specialist frameworks unless the user explicitly requests a combined analysis.

## 4. BASIL architectural contract

CRSE is an optional BRIAN Strategic Evaluation Skill.

- **MANUEL** supplies preserved source and Interaction Evidence and remains responsible for source fidelity, transcription, diarisation, acoustic evidence and reconciliation.
- **BRIAN** owns interpretation, behavioural/relational meaning, value diagnosis and strategic response design.
- **CRSE** supplies a Carnegie-Rory reading inside BRIAN when explicitly invoked or deliberately selected.
- **Other Strategic Evaluation Skills** remain independent. Preserve disagreement until evidence resolves it.
- **Persistent relationship/network state** may receive verified facts and sufficiently supported longitudinal observations only, not speculative motives or value hypotheses as facts.
- **FAWLTY** may compare predictions, recommended interventions and actual outcomes to calibrate recurring value and engagement assumptions.
- **BASIL execution** may operationalise an accepted recommendation into communication, tasking, monitoring, proposition redesign or an experiment. Analysis does not equal execution.

## 5. Invocation and inputs

Run CRSE when the user asks for Carnegie-Rory Strategic Evaluation, Carnegie-Rory lens/review or equivalent.

Accept where available:

1. **Focal material** — latest email, message, meeting, call, document, incident or proposition.
2. **People involved** — names and roles.
3. **Objective** — what the user wants to understand, achieve or optimise.
4. **Optional priorities** — relationship, trust, response, persuasion, commercial value, adoption, repair, positioning, learning or another explicit priority.
5. **Depth** — RAPID, STANDARD or DEEP.

Retrieve missing context BASIL can access before asking the user to repeat it.

## 6. Depth profiles

### RAPID
For immediate decisions. Minimum output:

- decisive interpretation;
- principal voluntary-engagement/value insight;
- main friction or resistance;
- user's largest contribution or error;
- best low-cost asymmetric intervention;
- three compact optimisation objectives and strategies;
- recommended next move;
- one friction to remove, one value to preserve and one action to avoid.

### STANDARD
Default. Perform relevant evidence reconstruction, focal event analysis, longitudinal comparison where useful, Human Value Model, friction/activation diagnosis, user-side assessment, reframing/psychological arbitrage, key tests, exactly three optimisation objectives and three strategies, comparative decision and recommendation.

### DEEP
Use for high-stakes, evidence-rich, multi-party or longitudinal situations. Add:

- comprehensive evidence reconstruction and chronology;
- repeated engagement/follow-through patterns;
- competing causal explanations;
- stronger value architecture across stakeholders;
- proposition/relationship phase changes;
- disconfirming evidence;
- experiment and prediction register;
- sequencing and transition conditions between strategies.

If no mode is supplied, use the lightest mode that still answers the actual decision problem.

## 7. Evidence reconstruction gate

Before substantive analysis, locate and review materially relevant accessible evidence, including where available:

- current conversation and attachments;
- BASIL Project files and sources;
- Google Drive or connected storage;
- previous meetings, transcripts, recordings, notes and summaries;
- prior intelligence reports, behavioural profiles or strategic analyses;
- previous emails, messages and correspondence;
- proposals, presentations and exchanged documents;
- earlier recommendations and predictions;
- commitments, decisions and actual follow-through;
- relevant organisational, commercial or relationship context.

Use names, organisations, dates, document titles and related terminology.

Direct evidence outranks previous analysis. Earlier reports remain analytical history and hypotheses unless independently supported.

Establish internally:

- evidence found and reviewed;
- material evidence inaccessible;
- relevant chronology;
- previous expectations and commitments;
- recurring enthusiasm, resistance, delay or friction;
- apparent changes in engagement;
- earlier hypotheses now testable;
- unresolved intelligence gaps.

If access is limited, state the limitation precisely and continue with the strongest material available.

## 8. Analytical discipline

Use:

`EVIDENCE -> OBSERVATION -> INTERPRETATION -> COMPETING EXPLANATION -> VALUE IMPLICATION -> BEHAVIOURAL CONSEQUENCE`

Where action or testing matters, extend to:

`... -> INTERVENTION / TEST -> PREDICTED RESPONSE -> DISCONFIRMING INDICATOR`

Distinguish:

- genuine interest vs politeness;
- warmth vs commitment;
- appreciation vs flattery;
- listening vs passive agreement;
- stated reasons vs operative causes;
- objective value vs experienced value;
- functional benefit vs psychological benefit;
- liking vs willingness to act;
- low motivation vs excessive friction;
- useful reassurance vs unnecessary persuasion;
- meaningful friction vs meaningless obstruction;
- autonomy vs indecision;
- status signalling vs actual preference;
- positive sentiment vs behavioural activation;
- communication failure vs proposition failure.

For important interpretations consider simple explanations before complex psychological ones. Do not attach named biases merely because they resemble the facts.

## 9. Carnegie analytical engine

Where evidence supports them, examine:

- self-command and ego regulation;
- perspective-taking;
- genuine interest;
- listening quality;
- recognition and significance;
- dignity and face;
- criticism and defensiveness;
- autonomy and voluntary cooperation;
- ownership of ideas;
- interests beneath positions;
- sincere appreciation;
- constructive identity;
- encouragement and perceived efficacy;
- emotional salience;
- audience-centred communication;
- stories, examples and vivid expression;
- whether correction addresses behaviour without unnecessarily attacking identity.

Carnegie does not mean permanent agreeableness. Respect does not require agreement. Face-saving does not require tolerating misconduct. Avoiding unnecessary argument does not require abandoning truth.

## 10. Rory analytical engine

Analyse five realities when materially relevant:

1. **Functional reality** — what objectively happens, costs, constrains or creates practical value.
2. **Experienced reality** — anxiety, ease, reassurance, confusion, anticipation, control and felt quality.
3. **Social reality** — identity, status, belonging, signalling and reputational consequences.
4. **Decision reality** — uncertainty, reference class, satisficing, regret, effort and defensibility.
5. **Organisational reality** — what is politically/professionally safe to decide, justify or reject.

Also examine:

- psychological value;
- signalling;
- certainty and perceived control;
- framing and semantics;
- reference classes;
- hidden functions;
- satisficing;
- meaningful friction;
- choice complexity;
- attention and salience;
- apparent irrationality as evidence of an incomplete model;
- anomalies and outliers;
- trivial details with nonlinear effects;
- psychological arbitrage;
- low-cost asymmetric interventions;
- explore vs exploit;
- counter-intuitive alternatives;
- whether the inherited problem definition is wrong.

A Rory construct earns inclusion only when it changes what should be tested, framed, built, removed, preserved or communicated.

## 11. Standard analytical workflow

### 11.1 Executive Summary
Complete last, place first. In STANDARD/DEEP target roughly 250–400 words.

Summarise:

- what most probably happened;
- how the other party most likely experienced it;
- most important relationship dynamic;
- most important value dynamic;
- principal source of friction/resistance;
- whether actual behaviour matches stated interest;
- what the user did that increased or reduced value;
- main opportunity;
- main risk/uncertainty;
- three optimisation objectives and corresponding strategies;
- key trade-off in each;
- currently recommended strategy and why;
- how the recommendation changes if the true objective differs;
- single highest-value next move.

### 11.2 Focal Event Analysis
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the 2–4 conclusions that matter most.

Analyse:

- tone and interpersonal experience;
- attention and recognition;
- perspective-taking;
- dignity and autonomy;
- apparent interest and engagement;
- questions, objections and omissions;
- what appears personally meaningful vs merely functional;
- uncertainty and perceived risk;
- ownership of the discussion or solution;
- status/signalling effects;
- framing and reference class;
- effort required to understand or act;
- whether next action was psychologically and practically clear;
- what the user contributed positively and negatively;
- what the interaction most likely caused the other party to think, feel and do.

Do not confuse a pleasant interaction with a successful interaction.

### 11.3 Contextual / Longitudinal Analysis
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: what becomes visible only across the history.

Determine:

- engagement trend: increasing, stable or declining;
- whether goodwill translates into behaviour;
- recurring sources of value and friction;
- what repeatedly generates interest, delay or avoidance;
- whether the other party appears to feel understood;
- expectations created, fulfilled or disappointed;
- whether progression became easier or harder;
- whether either party became too transactional or too accommodating;
- predictability of the interaction;
- small prior interventions with disproportionate effects;
- relationship/proposition phase change;
- earlier interpretations confirmed, weakened or missed.

Explain trajectory, not chronology for its own sake.

### 11.4 Human Value Model
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the shortest useful explanation of why the other party currently values, tolerates or resists the interaction/proposition.

Map:

- **Functional value** — materially useful outcome.
- **Psychological value** — reassurance, confidence, enjoyment, meaning, anticipation or control.
- **Social value** — identity, status, belonging, competence or reputation.
- **Informational value** — uncertainty/ambiguity/decision burden removed or created.
- **Relationship value** — trust, recognition, access, reciprocity and future optionality.

Distinguish what the person says matters, what behaviour suggests matters, what is necessary, what is sufficient and what appears psychologically mispriced. Preserve the strongest credible competing explanation.

### 11.5 Friction, Resistance and Behavioural Activation
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the smallest number of factors most likely preventing the desired behaviour.

Start with:

> What specifically do I want this person to do next, and why have they not done it already?

Assess:

- insufficient motivation;
- unclear value;
- uncertainty or ambiguity;
- excessive effort;
- status/dignity risk;
- weak ownership;
- too much commitment too early;
- decision complexity;
- regret risk;
- timing;
- lack of reassurance;
- hidden objections;
- organisational defensibility;
- absence of urgency;
- weak salience;
- meaningless friction.

Then ask whether any friction usefully signals commitment, seriousness, scarcity, quality, ownership, exclusivity, effort or credibility. Distinguish obstruction from meaningful effort; do not mechanically remove friction.

### 11.6 My Performance and Effect on the System
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the one or two user-side changes most likely to improve the outcome.

Evaluate whether the user:

- regulated ego and emotional reaction;
- showed genuine interest and listened;
- understood the other perspective before advocating;
- recognised genuine value in the other's contribution;
- preserved dignity and autonomy;
- argued unnecessarily or over-explained;
- corrected things that did not need correcting;
- made the interaction too self-centred;
- created or reduced ownership;
- translated the proposition into the other's interests;
- made objective value psychologically visible;
- chose the correct comparison/reference class;
- created reassurance and reduced uncertainty;
- used credible signals of effort/seriousness;
- made next action clear;
- asked for too much too soon;
- mistook objective superiority for psychological attractiveness;
- increased communication after marginal value had turned negative.

Also identify what should be deliberately preserved.

### 11.7 Reframing and Psychological Arbitrage
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the intervention with the best potential behavioural effect relative to cost, effort or relationship risk.

Before improving the existing approach, challenge the inherited problem:

- Are we solving the correct problem?
- Is the optimised variable the variable actually experienced?
- Is the difficulty functional or psychological?
- Is the stated objection the operative objection?
- Is the current reference class helping or hurting?
- Does language place the proposition in the wrong category?
- What trivial factor might matter disproportionately?
- What hidden function would disappear if the interaction became perfectly efficient?
- What behaviour contradicts the stated explanation?
- Under what circumstances would the opposite approach work?
- What would we test if reputational embarrassment were impossible?

Retain only high-value possibilities: reframing, reference-class change, wording, sequence, timing, certainty, agency, smaller initial commitment, friction removal/preservation, recognition, making intangible value visible, stronger signals, distinctive gestures, reducing unnecessary communication, psychological safety or category redesign.

Separate interesting hypothesis from supported recommendation.

### 11.8 Key Unknowns and Tests
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the uncertainty that most limits confident action.

For each high-value unknown state:

`UNKNOWN / HYPOTHESIS -> WHY IT MATTERS -> LEADING EXPLANATIONS -> CHEAPEST/NATURAL TEST -> HOW EACH RESULT CHANGES STRATEGY`

Prefer ordinary future behaviour and natural interaction over conspicuous testing.

### 11.9 Exactly Three Optimisation Objectives
Before designing strategy, question the assumption about what success means.

Identify exactly three plausible optimisation objectives. At least one must reflect the explicit objective where one exists. The other two must be credible alternatives from the evidence.

For each state:

- **OBJECTIVE** — what successful optimisation means.
- **WHY PLAUSIBLE** — why it could be the true priority.
- **WHAT IT PRIORITISES** — what matters most.
- **WHAT IT SACRIFICES** — what may rationally be delayed, reduced or lost.
- **CRITICAL ASSUMPTION** — what must be true for this objective to be correct.

Do not choose between them yet.

### 11.10 Three Genuinely Different Forward Strategies
Create exactly one strategy per objective. They must differ in purpose, behavioural mechanism, posture, resource allocation, timing and accepted trade-off.

Use this test: if each strategy succeeded perfectly, would each produce a materially different outcome? If not, redesign them.

For each:

**STRATEGY A/B/C — [OPTIMISATION OBJECTIVE]**

Include:

- objective being optimised;
- strategic thesis;
- what it deliberately prioritises and does not optimise;
- Carnegie logic;
- Rory logic;
- interpersonal posture;
- perceived-value logic;
- immediate next action;
- what to emphasise and reduce;
- what not to argue;
- what remains unsaid for now;
- what the other person should ideally understand, feel and do;
- dignity/agency preservation;
- uncertainty to reduce;
- friction to remove and preserve;
- asymmetric low-cost intervention worth testing;
- likely response;
- advantages, disadvantages and opportunity cost;
- principal risk if the objective assumption is wrong;
- indicators of genuine engagement;
- indicators of politeness without meaningful movement;
- transition or stopping condition.

Use example wording only where it adds material value.

### 11.11 Comparative Decision and Recommended Path
Compare the strategies only on criteria that matter in this case, such as objective fit, behavioural movement, relationship effect, perceived-value effect, trust, information gain, reversibility, cost/effort, time horizon, downside, future optionality and opportunity cost. Do not manufacture numerical precision.

Then state **CURRENTLY RECOMMENDED STRATEGY** and explain:

- what objective the user most probably wants to optimise;
- why it appears more likely than the other two;
- why the recommended strategy therefore dominates;
- what it gains and consciously sacrifices;
- assumptions on which it depends;
- evidence that would cause the recommendation to change.

Then explicitly state:

- **IF THE TRUE PRIORITY IS [OBJECTIVE B]:** Strategy B becomes preferable because ...
- **IF THE TRUE PRIORITY IS [OBJECTIVE C]:** Strategy C becomes preferable because ...

If sequencing is superior, specify starting strategy, transition trigger, next strategy and stop condition. Do not recommend a hybrid merely to avoid making a decision.

Conclude:

**NEXT MOVE:** the single highest-value action now.  
**REMOVE THIS FRICTION:** one unnecessary barrier most worth eliminating.  
**PRESERVE THIS VALUE:** one existing feature, behaviour or signal that should not be optimised away.  
**DO NOT DO:** one action most likely to reduce voluntary engagement or destroy value.

## 13. Quality gates

Before finalising verify:

- direct evidence outranks prior analysis and theory;
- genuine interest is distinguished from politeness and goodwill from follow-through;
- structural/proposition failure has been considered before assuming a communication problem;
- simple explanations have been considered before complex psychological ones;
- Carnegie has not been reduced to agreeableness or flattery;
- Rory has not become bias bingo, performative contrarianism or anecdotal proof;
- objective and experienced value are separated;
- useful friction is not automatically removed;
- persuasion is not assumed to be the solution to all resistance;
- the user's own contribution is assessed;
- the inherited problem definition has been challenged where useful;
- three objectives define materially different successes;
- three strategies would produce materially different outcomes if successful;
- the recommendation follows the most likely actual objective rather than generic relationship preservation;
- no section exists merely to demonstrate framework knowledge.

## 14. Handoff and learning

Return four classes of downstream material:

1. **Validated factual updates** — eligible for canonical state when independently supported.
2. **Working engagement/value hypotheses** — remain inside BRIAN until validated or rejected.
3. **Predictions/tests** — candidate inputs for future reconciliation and FAWLTY calibration.
4. **Candidate interventions/experiments** — remain proposals until explicitly accepted and executed.

When subsequent evidence arrives, compare predicted engagement, friction and value effects against actual behaviour. Record confirmed, weakened, contradicted and unresolved hypotheses without retrospective narrative protection.
