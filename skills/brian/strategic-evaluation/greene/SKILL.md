# BASIL | BRIAN | Greene Strategic Evaluation

**Short name:** GSE  
**Version:** 0.1 WORKING  
**Status:** Specialist Strategic Evaluation Skill. Not TESTED, ACCEPTED or LOCKED.  
**Placement:** `BASIL -> BRIAN -> Strategic Evaluation Skills -> Greene Strategic Evaluation`

## 1. Mission

Use Robert Greene's work on power, human nature, character, strategy and mastery as the primary lens for understanding a consequential situation and choosing a course of action.

The governing question is:

> What strategic game is actually being played, consciously or otherwise, where does real power rest, what are the parties protecting or seeking, how is the position changing and what course of action improves the user's position without sacrificing the larger objective for a minor tactical victory?

Analyse the strategic system, not only the counterpart: incentives, dependencies, access, reputation, alternatives, alliances, initiative, timing, terrain, options, countermoves and the user's own conduct and vulnerabilities.

Do not impersonate Robert Greene, imitate his literary voice or mechanically apply individual laws. Treat Greene's principles as context-dependent strategic propositions that must survive comparison with evidence.

## 2. Source basis and boundaries

Primary source family:

- *The Laws of Human Nature* — recurring character patterns, irrationality, masks/persona, self-opinion, envy, aggression, grandiosity, conformity and self-awareness.
- *The 48 Laws of Power* — dependency, reputation, status, concealment, access, scarcity, attention, leverage, political behaviour and option control.
- *The 33 Strategies of War* — conflict structure, initiative, tempo, timing, indirect action, terrain, centres of gravity, escalation, strategic patience, countermoves and exit conditions.
- *Mastery* — competence, apprenticeship, tacit knowledge, mentorship, social intelligence, craft and long-term strategic maturation where relevant.

Use first-party Greene interviews, lectures or authoritative source material when accessible. Earlier BASIL profiles and Greene-inspired analyses are analytical history, not source-of-truth evidence.

Do not:

- use archetypes as diagnoses;
- treat any law as universally true;
- militarise ordinary disagreement;
- assume hidden hostility because the framework makes it imaginable;
- equate manipulation with strategy;
- treat formal title, warmth, access or attention as proof of actual power or commitment.

## 3. BASIL architectural contract

GSE is an optional BRIAN Strategic Evaluation Skill.

- **MANUEL** owns source preservation, transcription, diarisation, acoustic evidence, speaker reconciliation and canonical Interaction Evidence.
- **BRIAN** owns interpretation, strategic meaning, power analysis, relational intelligence, hypothesis formation and response design.
- **GSE** supplies a Greene-centred reading inside BRIAN when explicitly invoked or deliberately selected.
- **Other Strategic Evaluation Skills** remain independent. Preserve disagreement and identify the evidential reason rather than averaging conclusions.
- **Persistent relationship/network state** may receive verified facts and sufficiently supported longitudinal observations only. Do not persist speculative motives, archetypal labels, predicted hostility or unvalidated power hypotheses as facts.
- **FAWLTY** may compare predicted countermoves and recommendations with later evidence to calibrate the lens.
- **BASIL execution** may operationalise an accepted recommendation, but analysis is not execution.

Do not import Hughes, Carnegie-Rory or another specialist framework unless the user explicitly requests a combined analysis.

## 4. Invocation and inputs

Run GSE when the user asks for a Greene analysis, Greene lens, Greene Strategic Evaluation, power/position review or equivalent.

Accept where available:

1. **Focal material** — latest email, message, meeting, call, document, incident or proposition.
2. **People involved** — names and roles.
3. **Objective** — what the user wants to understand, achieve or optimise.
4. **Optional priorities** — relationship, position, influence, information, negotiation, reputation, access, autonomy, conflict, long-term advantage or another explicit priority.
5. **Depth** — RAPID, STANDARD or DEEP.

Retrieve context BASIL can access before asking the user to repeat it.

## 5. Depth profiles

### RAPID
For immediate strategic decisions. Minimum output:

- decisive interpretation of the focal event;
- current power implication;
- probable operative interest;
- user's strongest current advantage;
- user's largest vulnerability;
- most likely countermove;
- three compact strategies;
- recommended next move;
- single action to avoid.

### STANDARD
Default. Use relevant evidence reconstruction, focal analysis, longitudinal comparison where useful, power map, character/persona/interest model, user-side assessment, likely countermoves, key tests, three strategies and recommendation.

### DEEP
Use for high-stakes, multi-actor, conflict-rich or longitudinal cases. Add:

- comprehensive evidence reconstruction and chronology;
- longitudinal power evolution;
- alliance/coalition analysis;
- centres of gravity;
- competing hypotheses and disconfirming evidence;
- dependency and alternatives mapping;
- second- and third-order consequences;
- prediction register;
- transition conditions between strategies.

If no mode is supplied, use the lightest mode that still answers the actual strategic problem.

## 6. Evidence reconstruction gate

Before substantive analysis, locate and review materially relevant accessible evidence, including where available:

- current conversation and attachments;
- BASIL Project files and sources;
- Google Drive or connected storage;
- previous meetings, transcripts, recordings, notes and summaries;
- prior behavioural or strategic profiles;
- intelligence reports and dossiers;
- previous emails, messages and correspondence;
- pre-meeting briefs and post-interaction analyses;
- agreements, proposals, decisions and commitments;
- documents produced by either party;
- relevant organisational, commercial or political context;
- prior predictions, strategic recommendations and unresolved questions.

Use names, organisations, dates, aliases, document titles and associated terminology for retrieval.

Direct evidence outranks previous interpretation. Earlier reports remain hypotheses and analytical history unless independently supported.

Establish internally:

- evidence found and actually reviewed;
- material evidence unavailable;
- relevant chronology;
- earlier hypotheses and predictions;
- commitments made, fulfilled or broken;
- shifts in access, attention, reciprocity or dependency;
- conflicts, concessions, delays and position changes;
- unresolved intelligence gaps.

If access prevents full reconstruction, state the precise limitation and continue with the strongest evidence available.

## 7. Analytical discipline

Use:

`EVIDENCE -> OBSERVATION -> INTERPRETATION -> COMPETING EXPLANATION -> POWER IMPLICATION -> STRATEGIC CONSEQUENCE`

When future behaviour matters, extend to:

`... -> PREDICTION -> DISCONFIRMING INDICATOR / STRATEGIC TEST`

Distinguish:

- public position vs operative interest;
- character vs temporary emotion;
- persona vs underlying pattern;
- politeness vs allegiance;
- attention vs commitment;
- status vs actual power;
- title vs decision authority;
- access vs influence;
- dependence vs loyalty;
- cooperation vs alignment;
- concession vs weakness;
- silence vs surrender;
- tactical victory vs strategic gain;
- deliberate strategy vs behaviour produced by circumstance.

Prefer recurring patterns, incentives, costly choices, dependencies and changes over time. Use simple explanations before elaborate strategic narratives where both fit the facts.

## 8. Greene analytical engine

Use only constructs that improve explanation, discrimination, prediction or strategic choice.

Where evidence supports them, examine:

- recurring character patterns and compulsive behaviour;
- persona, masks and impression management;
- declared intentions vs repeated conduct;
- self-interest and operative incentives;
- irrationality and emotional contamination;
- defensiveness and protected self-opinion;
- envy, resentment, hostility and passive aggression;
- grandiosity and overestimation;
- aggression and conflict style;
- conformity and group pressure;
- reputation and reputation management;
- status, hierarchy and rank sensitivity;
- dependency and independence;
- access, scarcity and attention;
- concealment and selective disclosure;
- leverage and centres of gravity;
- who controls options and who reacts;
- alliances and coalition structure;
- initiative, tempo and strategic patience;
- timing, waiting and premature action;
- terrain: where each party is strongest or weakest;
- direct vs indirect approaches;
- concentration vs dispersion of effort;
- escalation/de-escalation and costs of conflict;
- countermoves and second-order consequences;
- exit conditions and ability to end cleanly;
- long-term position vs immediate gratification;
- mastery, competence and social intelligence where relevant.

Do not force framework completeness. Name a Greene principle only when it sharpens the actual analysis.

## 9. Standard analytical workflow

### 9.1 Executive Summary
Complete last, place first. In STANDARD/DEEP target roughly 250–400 words.

Summarise:

- what most probably happened strategically;
- what the focal event changed;
- actual power relationship;
- probable operative interests;
- most important character/persona/reputation dynamic;
- user's present strategic position;
- principal opportunity;
- principal vulnerability;
- three forward strategies;
- recommended strategy for the current optimisation objective;
- single highest-value next move.

### 9.2 Focal Event Analysis
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the 2–4 developments that most affect position, perception or future options.

Analyse:

- what each party wanted and protected;
- who initiated, reacted, pursued, delayed or redirected;
- offers, requests, refusals and ambiguity;
- concessions and their real value;
- changes in access, attention, commitment or reciprocity;
- use of status, authority or reputation;
- attempts to define frame or terms;
- selective disclosure or strategic ambiguity;
- pressure, defensiveness or loss of composure;
- discrepancies between words and incentives;
- who ended with more or fewer options.

Distinguish symbolic moves from consequences that materially alter the position.

### 9.3 Contextual / Longitudinal Strategic Analysis
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: what becomes visible only in the full history.

Determine:

- how the relationship and power structure evolved;
- recurring pursuit-withdrawal or initiative-response patterns;
- who historically sets timing and agenda;
- changes in responsiveness, access and dependence;
- promises vs delivery;
- trust and reputation trajectory;
- whether concessions created reciprocity;
- where dependency increased or decreased;
- recurring conflicts or avoided issues;
- whether either side changed strategy;
- whether the user's strategy became predictable;
- continuation, deviation or phase transition;
- earlier analysis confirmed, weakened or missed.

Explain trajectory, not chronology for its own sake.

### 9.4 Power Map
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: where real power resides and why.

Assess:

- decision authority;
- information;
- access;
- expertise;
- reputation;
- legitimacy;
- resources;
- alternatives;
- time pressure;
- scarcity;
- relationships and alliances;
- ability to confer or withhold value;
- ability to walk away;
- dependence and replaceability.

Identify what each party needs, what can be obtained elsewhere, asymmetric dependencies, hidden constraints, centres of gravity and who has the stronger practical alternative if the engagement fails. Explain apparent vs actual power.

### 9.5 Character, Persona and Operative Interests
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the shortest useful description of the principal other's recurring strategic logic.

Where supported, assess:

- recurring character pattern;
- presented persona and protected self-image;
- cultivated reputation and status sensitivities;
- habitual control methods;
- response to criticism, obstruction, dependency or uncertainty;
- preferred conflict form;
- strategic patience;
- directness, indirection, concealment and signalling;
- recurring self-interest;
- stated principles vs costly choices;
- conditions under which behaviour changes.

For each major interpretation preserve the strongest credible alternative.

### 9.6 My Strategic Performance and Position
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the one or two aspects of user conduct that most strengthened or weakened position.

Evaluate whether the user:

- revealed too much or too little;
- pursued when waiting would improve leverage;
- waited when initiative was required;
- confused warmth with commitment;
- conceded without reciprocal value;
- became predictable;
- overplayed or underplayed status;
- allowed the other party to define all options;
- defended reputation unnecessarily;
- argued when demonstration would be stronger;
- sought validation instead of strategic outcome;
- reacted emotionally to provocation, silence or ambiguity;
- concentrated effort on the decisive issue;
- protected independence and future options;
- recognised diminishing returns.

Also identify what should be preserved.

### 9.7 Strategic Environment and Likely Countermoves
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the most likely response if the user materially changes approach.

Assess:

- what the other party expects next;
- what happens if the user complies or departs from that expectation;
- likely responses to pressure, reduced pursuit, greater independence, direct challenge, strategic patience or a new offer;
- third parties or alliances that could change the game;
- reputational consequences;
- escalation risks;
- timing opportunities;
- when non-action is superior;
- conditions for ending the engagement.

Think at least two moves beyond the immediate action without pretending certainty.

### 9.8 Key Unknowns and Strategic Tests
Open with **STRATEGIC INSIGHT**: the uncertainty that most prevents confident strategic choice.

For each high-value unknown use:

`UNKNOWN / HYPOTHESIS -> WHY IT MATTERS -> COMPETING EXPLANATIONS -> NATURAL TEST / OBSERVATION -> STRATEGIC CONSEQUENCE OF EACH RESULT`

Avoid conspicuous tests merely for analytical neatness.

### 9.9 Three Forward Strategies
Create exactly three genuinely different strategic postures.

Infer objectives from:

1. explicit user objective;
2. relationship history;
3. present power structure;
4. available alternatives;
5. downside if the interpretation is wrong.

Possible optimisation targets include relationship/coalition value, leverage/independence, rapid progress/commitment, reputation/positioning, information with optionality or disengagement/reduced exposure.

For each:

**STRATEGY A/B/C — [STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE]**

Include:

- strategic thesis;
- what it optimises;
- relevant Greene logic;
- power mechanism;
- position/frame;
- what to do and stop doing;
- what remains undisclosed;
- what should be demonstrated rather than argued;
- timing;
- expected countermove;
- second-order consequences;
- advantages, disadvantages and opportunity cost;
- principal failure mode;
- success indicators;
- withdrawal, escalation or change indicators.

### 9.10 Recommended Path
Select the strategy with the highest expected strategic value for the actual objective.

Explain:

- why it outranks the alternatives;
- trade-off being accepted;
- assumptions on which it depends;
- most likely countermove;
- downside if the model is wrong;
- evidence that would make another strategy superior.

If sequencing is better, specify:

`INITIAL STRATEGY -> TRANSITION CONDITION -> NEXT STRATEGY -> STOP CONDITION`

Conclude:

**NEXT MOVE:** one highest-value action now.  
**DO NOT DO:** one action most likely to damage the position.

## 10. Output architecture

Return clean Markdown with:

1. Executive Summary
2. Focal Event Analysis
3. Contextual / Longitudinal Strategic Analysis
4. Power Map
5. Character, Persona and Operative Interests
6. My Strategic Performance and Position
7. Strategic Environment and Likely Countermoves
8. Key Unknowns and Strategic Tests
9. Three Forward Strategies
10. Recommended Path
11. Evidence Boundary / Access Limitations — only if materially relevant

Place **STRATEGIC INSIGHT** first in every substantive section. Support three reading depths: Executive Summary, Strategic Insight blocks and Full Analysis.

## 11. Quality gates

Before finalising verify:

- direct evidence outranks framework expectation and earlier profiles;
- titles, warmth, access and attention are not confused with power or commitment;
- ordinary disagreement has not been militarised;
- hidden hostility has not been invented;
- no law has been treated as universally true;
- incentives, dependencies and costly choices have been tested against rhetoric;
- tactical victory has been separated from long-term position;
- plausible countermoves and second-order effects have been considered;
- competing explanations remain visible;
- the user's own strategic behaviour has been assessed;
- three strategies represent real trade-offs;
- the recommendation follows the actual optimisation objective;
- no section exists merely to display Greene terminology.

## 12. Handoff and learning

Return three classes of downstream material:

1. **Validated factual updates** — eligible for canonical relationship state when independently supported.
2. **Working strategic hypotheses** — remain inside BRIAN analysis until validated or rejected.
3. **Predictions/tests** — candidate inputs for future reconciliation and FAWLTY calibration.

When later evidence arrives, compare expected countermoves and outcomes against reality. Record confirmed, weakened, contradicted and unresolved hypotheses without rewriting prior predictions.
