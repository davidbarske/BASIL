# BASIL | BRIAN | Hughes Strategic Evaluation

**Short name:** HSE  
**Version:** 0.1 WORKING  
**Status:** Specialist Strategic Evaluation Skill. Does not supersede BASIL Meeting Intelligence, Pre-Engagement Intelligence, MANUEL evidence processing or other BRIAN lenses.  
**Proposed placement:** `BASIL -> BRIAN -> Strategic Evaluation Skills -> Hughes Strategic Evaluation`

## 1. Mission

Use Chase Hughes's behavioural-analysis methods as the primary analytical lens for understanding a focal interaction and deciding what to do next.

The unit of analysis is the **interaction system**: what each party communicated, perceived, reinforced, resisted, elicited or changed in the other. The skill analyses both the counterpart(s) and the user's own behaviour.

The skill may be applied to:

- email or message exchanges;
- meetings or calls;
- documents produced by either party;
- negotiations;
- incidents or relationship changes;
- sequences of interactions where longitudinal comparison matters.

Do not impersonate Chase Hughes or invent his views. Apply only concepts supported by accessible Hughes source material or by the skill's established analytical vocabulary.

## 2. BASIL architectural contract

HSE is an **optional BRIAN Strategic Evaluation Skill**, not a source-of-truth subsystem.

- **MANUEL** owns capture, source preservation, transcription, diarisation, acoustic evidence, speaker reconciliation and canonical interaction evidence.
- **BRIAN** owns interpretation, behavioural/relational reasoning, strategic meaning and response design.
- **HSE** is invoked inside BRIAN when a Hughes-centred reading is useful or explicitly requested.
- **Network** may receive only validated facts or sufficiently supported longitudinal observations. Do not persist HSE hypotheses, remote profiles or speculative motives as facts.
- **FAWLTY** may later compare HSE predictions with actual outcomes to calibrate recurring inference quality.
- HSE never silently replaces the conclusions of Meeting Intelligence or another specialist lens. Where outputs conflict, preserve the conflict and identify the evidential reason.
- Do not import Greene, Carnegie-Rory or other specialist frameworks into HSE unless the user explicitly requests a combined analysis.

## 3. Invocation rule

Run HSE when the user explicitly asks for a Hughes analysis, Hughes lens, Chase Hughes review, behavioural interaction review or invokes this skill by name.

Do not auto-run HSE merely because an interaction contains psychology, influence or negotiation. It is a deliberate specialist lens.

Accept four primary inputs where available:

1. **Focal material** — latest email, message, meeting, call, document or incident.
2. **People involved** — names and roles.
3. **Objective** — what the user wants to understand, achieve or optimise.
4. **Optional priority** — relationship, influence, information, negotiation, positioning, decision, risk or another explicit objective.

Retrieve missing context before asking the user to repeat information that BASIL can access.

## 4. Depth profiles

### RAPID
Use when the user needs an immediate decision or response and broad reconstruction would create more delay than value.

Minimum output:
- decisive reading of the focal event;
- strongest behavioural change or signal;
- user's main strength/error;
- principal risk or unknown;
- three compact options;
- recommended next move.

### STANDARD
Default for consequential interactions.

Perform focal-event analysis, relevant evidence reconstruction, longitudinal comparison where available, behavioural model, user-side analysis, key tests, three strategies and recommendation.

### DEEP
Use when stakes are high, history is substantial, multiple actors matter, the interaction may change a relationship or decision system, or the user asks for maximum depth.

Add:
- broader connected-source reconstruction;
- prior behavioural/intelligence products;
- explicit chronology;
- baseline/deviation analysis across multiple interactions;
- competing hypotheses and disconfirming evidence;
- participant-by-participant modelling where necessary;
- prediction register and future tests.

If no mode is supplied, infer the lightest mode that still answers the actual decision problem.

## 5. Evidence reconstruction gate

Before substantive contextual or longitudinal analysis, identify and review all materially relevant accessible evidence.

Search, where tools permit:

- current conversation and attachments;
- BASIL Project files and Project sources;
- Google Drive or other connected storage;
- previous meeting transcripts, recordings, notes and summaries;
- prior behavioural profiles or X-Ray profiles;
- intelligence reports, dossiers and research packs;
- previous emails, messages and correspondence;
- earlier pre-meeting briefs, interaction analyses and post-meeting debriefs;
- documents produced by either party;
- relevant chronology, commitments, decisions and follow-up records.

Use subject names, organisations, dates, correspondence titles, known aliases and related terminology for retrieval.

Direct evidence outranks prior analysis. Previous profiles and intelligence reports remain hypotheses and analytical history unless independently supported.

Before proceeding, establish internally:

- evidence found;
- evidence actually reviewed;
- evidence unavailable or inaccessible;
- relevant chronology;
- previous behavioural hypotheses;
- prior predictions worth testing;
- unresolved intelligence gaps.

If comprehensive retrieval is impossible, state the precise limitation and continue using the strongest available evidence.

## 6. Evidence and inference discipline

Use the analytical chain:

`EVIDENCE -> OBSERVATION -> INFERENCE -> COMPETING EXPLANATION -> STRATEGIC IMPLICATION`

Where prediction or diagnosis matters, extend it to:

`... -> LIVE TEST / DISCONFIRMING INDICATOR`

Treat confidence using these categories when useful:

- **Confirmed fact** — direct authoritative evidence.
- **Strongly supported inference** — multiple convergent observations with no stronger competing explanation.
- **Plausible working hypothesis** — useful and supported, but not established.
- **Competing hypothesis** — credible alternative that remains live.
- **Unknown** — evidence insufficient.

Do not flatten these into false certainty.

Before interpreting deviation, establish the best available baseline.

Prefer:
- behavioural clusters over isolated cues;
- repeated language over one-off wording;
- consequential choices over low-value mannerisms;
- topic-linked changes over generic body-language claims;
- actual behaviour over framework expectation.

Distinguish where relevant:
- state vs trait;
- authentic preference vs role behaviour;
- spontaneous behaviour vs tactic or impression management;
- politeness vs agreement;
- confidence vs competence;
- discomfort vs deception;
- stated motive vs operative driver.

Never infer non-verbal behaviour from text-only material.

## 7. Hughes framework use

Use Chase Hughes as the primary framework **inside this lens**, while preserving BASIL's evidence-first doctrine.

Where the evidence supports them, draw from:

- behavioural baselining, clusters and deviations;
- Behavioral Table of Elements;
- Six-Minute X-Ray profiling;
- primary and secondary social needs and protected fears;
- Values Map and revealed values;
- Decision Map and decision friction;
- locus of control and responsibility attribution;
- authority, confidence, discipline, leadership, gratitude, enjoyment and composure;
- collapse, composure and posturing;
- Authority Triangle;
- FATE, PCP and Six-Axis models where materially applicable;
- linguistic and communication patterns;
- pronoun use and verbal indicators;
- non-verbal indicators only when the source medium directly supports them;
- rapport, comfort and social frame;
- elicitation dynamics and information leakage;
- resistance, reactance, objections and concealed disagreement;
- identity, status, recognition, acceptance, significance, intelligence, pity and strength/power dynamics;
- communication, observation and self-mastery advantages or failures;
- interaction sequencing and likely behavioural responses.

Do **not** force framework completeness. A Hughes construct earns inclusion only when it materially improves explanation, prediction, discrimination between hypotheses or strategy.

## 8. Analytical workflow

### 8.1 Executive synthesis
Complete this last but place it first in the output.

Summarise:
- what most probably happened;
- the most important behavioural/relational change;
- current best model of the counterpart or interaction system;
- most important conclusion about the user's performance;
- how the event fits the prior trajectory;
- principal opportunity;
- principal risk or unresolved uncertainty;
- the three strategic options;
- recommended option and why;
- single highest-value next move.

Target 250–400 words in DEEP/STANDARD. RAPID may be shorter.

### 8.2 Focal event analysis
Open with a **Strategic Insight** containing the 2–4 conclusions from this event that matter most.

Analyse:
- observable behaviour and language;
- authority, comfort and composure shifts;
- needs, values, fears, identity and status signals;
- decision signals, hesitation, commitment and friction;
- rapport and resistance;
- questions asked, avoided or redirected;
- information volunteered vs withheld;
- meaningful linguistic patterns;
- apparent expectations of each party;
- influence in both directions;
- what the user did effectively or ineffectively;
- what the counterpart may now believe about the user;
- positive evidence and anomalies.

### 8.3 Contextual / longitudinal analysis
Open with a **Strategic Insight** stating what becomes visible only when the focal event is compared with history.

Determine:
- baseline;
- deviations;
- recurring interaction patterns;
- recurring needs, sensitivities, values and decision behaviours;
- direction of rapport, trust, authority, interest and resistance;
- earlier predictions confirmed, weakened or contradicted;
- strategy changes by either party;
- whether the relationship has entered a new phase;
- what previous analysis got right;
- what previous analysis missed or misread.

Explain trajectory, not chronology for its own sake.

### 8.4 Behavioural model
Open with a **Strategic Insight** giving the shortest useful description of how the counterpart or interaction system currently appears to operate.

Build the smallest model that explains the greatest amount of evidence.

Where justified include:
- operative social needs and protected fears;
- protected identity;
- revealed values;
- authority and comfort profile;
- locus of control;
- decision mechanics;
- sources of resistance or reactance;
- preferred interaction role;
- reward, threat, status and recognition sensitivities;
- communication style;
- conditions under which behaviour changes materially.

For every major conclusion, include the strongest competing explanation where one remains credible.

### 8.5 User performance and effect on the system
Open with a **Strategic Insight** identifying the one or two changes in the user's behaviour most likely to improve outcomes.

Assess:
- authority;
- composure;
- comfort;
- communication;
- observation;
- pacing and timing;
- questioning;
- disclosure;
- framing;
- rapport;
- status signalling;
- responsiveness to behavioural feedback.

Identify where the user may have:
- created avoidable pressure;
- reduced authority;
- over-explained;
- collapsed;
- postured;
- missed information;
- generated reactance.

Also identify what should be preserved because it is working.

### 8.6 Key unknowns and next tests
Open with a **Strategic Insight** stating what must be learned before confidence can materially improve.

Retain only high-value unknowns.

For each unknown specify a natural future:
- observation;
- question;
- information request;
- interaction;
- behavioural test;

that can distinguish leading hypotheses without making the relationship artificial.

### 8.7 Three forward strategies
Create three **genuinely different** strategies that optimise different plausible objectives.

Infer objectives from:
1. explicit user objective;
2. interaction history;
3. user's apparent constraints and priorities;
4. current behavioural evidence.

Each strategy must contain:

**STRATEGY A/B/C — [OPTIMISATION OBJECTIVE]**

- strategic thesis;
- what it optimises;
- behavioural mechanism;
- frame/posture;
- immediate action;
- what to emphasise, disclose, ask or avoid;
- useful wording only when it creates real value;
- expected response;
- advantages;
- disadvantages and opportunity cost;
- success indicators;
- change-of-approach indicators.

Ordinarily make the trade-offs materially different, for example:
- relationship / rapport;
- information / learning;
- progress / commitment;
- authority / positioning;
- downside control;
- another objective actually relevant to the case.

### 8.8 Recommended path
Select the strategy with the best expected value for the user's apparent optimisation target.

Explain:
- why it outranks the other two;
- assumptions it depends on;
- evidence that would cause the recommendation to change.

If a hybrid is superior, specify exactly which elements to combine.

Conclude with:

**NEXT MOVE:** one action only.

## 9. Output architecture

Return a clean Markdown analytical document with these sections:

1. Executive Summary
2. Focal Event Analysis
3. Contextual / Longitudinal Analysis
4. Behavioural Model
5. My Performance and Effect on the System
6. Key Unknowns and Next Tests
7. Three Forward Strategies
8. Recommended Path
9. Evidence Boundary / Access Limitations — include only if materially relevant

Place a **Strategic Insight** block first in every substantive section so the output works at three reading depths:

1. Executive Summary — complete assessment and recommendation.
2. Strategic Insight blocks — section-level conclusions.
3. Full analysis — evidence, reasoning, competing interpretations and strategic detail.

Keep methodology recitation out of the body unless it affects the conclusion.

## 10. Quality gates

Before finalising, verify:

- The focal event was treated as evidence, not as the whole relationship.
- Available material evidence was retrieved before longitudinal analysis.
- Direct evidence outranks prior profiles and frameworks.
- Non-verbal claims are supported by actual non-verbal source media.
- Each major inference can be traced to evidence or labelled as hypothesis.
- Strong competing explanations have not been suppressed.
- The user's own behaviour has been analysed, not merely the counterpart.
- The three strategies represent real trade-offs.
- The recommendation follows the user's actual objective rather than generic relationship preservation.
- The NEXT MOVE is singular and executable.
- No HSE hypothesis has been silently promoted into BASIL canonical fact state.
- No section exists merely to demonstrate that a Hughes model was remembered.

## 11. Handoff and learning

HSE may return three classes of downstream material:

1. **Validated factual updates** — eligible for the canonical interaction/relationship record when independently supported.
2. **Working behavioural hypotheses** — remain inside BRIAN case analysis until future evidence validates or rejects them.
3. **Predictions/tests** — candidate inputs for post-interaction reconciliation and FAWLTY calibration.

After a subsequent interaction, compare predictions with actual evidence. Record confirmed, weakened, contradicted and unresolved hypotheses. Do not rewrite earlier predictions to make them appear correct.
