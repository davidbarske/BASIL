# Primary-source research — BASIL lab adaptation

1. Define the decision or question the research must resolve.
2. Prefer first-party documents, source code, specifications, official APIs, filings and original evidence over secondary summaries.
3. Trace material claims to the source that owns them.
4. Separate source-derived fact from analytical inference.
5. Record conflicts instead of averaging them away.
6. Persist reusable findings in the existing project/repository location with citations and provenance.
7. State what was not found or could not be verified.

Research is complete when the decision-relevant claims are evidence-backed, not when a fixed number of sources has been collected.
